How the forecast works (plain English)

We fit a classic time-series model (ARIMA) to past prices and extend the trend 30 business days out; the shaded band is the 95% confidence interval — wider means less sure. It cannot predict news, results or events. To judge trust, we replay history: hide the last 30 days, forecast them, and measure how far off we were. High = clearly better than assuming tomorrow equals today · Medium = about the same as that guess · Low = ignore the projection.

        What do these words mean? (30-second guide)

        • 1M change — price move over the last ~21 trading days. Momentum snapshot.
        • Volatility — how wildly the price swings, annualised. 0.15 is calm, 0.40+ is a rollercoaster.
        • Sharpe — return per unit of risk. Above 1 = genuinely good; below 0 = pain without pay.
        • Max drawdown — worst peak-to-trough fall. Your worst-case history lesson.
        • MA20 / MA50 — the average price over the last 20 trading days (about a month) / 50 days (about 2½ months). Think of them as the market's memory: price trading above its average means buyers have been in charge lately; below means sellers are.
        • 52-week high — highest price in a year. Far below it can mean a dip — or a reason.
        • Typical miss (±%) — average % the forecast was off when replayed on the last 30 days. Lower is better.
        • Forecast confidence — how far off the forecast would have been on the last 30 days. High = beats the simple guess "tomorrow = today" · Medium = no better · Low = ignore the projection.
        • Anomaly (z-score) — a daily move so big (>3 standard deviations) it stands out from the last 60 days.
        • Correlation — do two stocks move together? 1.0 = lockstep, 0 = independent. Low pairs diversify you.
